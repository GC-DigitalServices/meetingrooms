PHASE 8 — ROLLOUT
==================

Goal
----
Take the platform from a pilot of a few rooms to every bookable
room in the college. For a ~20-room estate, two waves is enough.
Hand over day-to-day operations to the IT team and establish the
post-launch support model.

Duration estimate
-----------------
~2 to 3 weeks, ideally not overlapping with the start of a term's
first week.


Prerequisites
-------------
- Phases 1 through 7 complete.
- Pilot rooms from phase 6 have been running for 2+ weeks with
  no major incidents.
- IT team trained on the runbooks from phase 7.
- MDM profile for iPads tested and ready to deploy at scale.


Rollout waves
-------------
Wave 1 — Early adopter rooms (3-5 rooms, week 1)
  Mix of room types: a standard meeting room, a study room
  available to students, the composite hall (or whichever
  partitionable room you have). Invite ~15 staff and ~10
  students as pilot users. Daily check-ins for 3 days, then
  every other day.

Wave 2 — All remaining rooms (week 2-3)
  All other rooms onboarded. Announce broadly via college
  comms. Drop-in support sessions per building during the
  first few days.

If the estate ever doubles (~40 rooms), split wave 2 into a
"half the estate" wave and a "rest" wave. For ~20 rooms this
is unnecessary.


Per-wave checklist
------------------
Before each wave, per room:
  - Room metadata reviewed in rooms.yaml and imported.
  - allowedGroups set correctly (and reviewed by an
    appropriate stakeholder, e.g. CLT for staff/admin rooms).
  - Mailbox lockdown applied (PowerShell from phase 1
    runbook) and verified — Outlook invite is auto-declined.
  - Application Access Policy includes the mailbox.
  - Graph subscription created (the onboarding flow handles
    this automatically once the room is in the database).
  - iPad(s) provisioned via MDM: app installed, device
    enrolled, wall-mounted, powered on. Scope selected
    during pairing (STANDARD / SECTION / COMPOSITE).
  - Pairing pass: pair-display flow run, verified with a
    test booking.
  - Wi-Fi signal checked at the mount location.

During each wave:
  - Admin dashboard watched daily by at least one engineer
    and one IT ops person.
  - Feedback form linked from the portal footer, triaged
    daily.
  - Incident log kept even for minor issues; reviewed at
    end-of-wave retro.

After each wave:
  - Retro: what worked, what didn't, what to fix before
    the next wave.
  - Runbooks updated with any new incidents.
  - Roadmap updated with genuinely requested features versus
    nice-to-haves we can defer.


Work items
----------
1. Communications plan
   - Email to all staff before wave 1 with a short demo video
     covering both portal and iPad.
   - Email to all students before wave 1 explaining what they
     can book and how (some study rooms? library rooms?).
     This is a separate email — students don't need to hear
     about meeting room booking processes.
   - One-page quick reference printed and placed in each
     staff room.
   - FAQ on the staff intranet (and a smaller one on the
     student intranet, scoped to what students can book).
   - Make clear in both: room bookings are not made in
     Outlook anymore. This is a real behaviour change for
     staff who previously invited rooms to meetings; the
     mailbox lockdown means those invites are now auto-
     declined.

2. Training
   - 15-minute Teams session recorded for staff, covering
     the portal and iPad QR flow.
   - Shorter clip for students.
   - Separate session for admins on device pairing, audit
     log, allowedGroups management.

3. Support model
   - Tier 1 (IT helpdesk): basic issues, reseating iPads,
     resetting device pairings.
   - Tier 2 (IT operations): runbooks, Graph subscription
     issues, allowedGroups corrections.
   - Tier 3 (dev / vendor): code-level issues.
   - No on-call rota at this scale. Office-hours support is
     sufficient; out-of-hours is best-effort. Revisit if a
     post-launch incident shows it's needed.

4. SLOs published to stakeholders
   - Portal availability: 99% during school hours.
   - Booking-write latency: p95 < 2s during school hours.
   - Booking-to-display propagation: < 5s during school
     hours.
   - iPad uptime: > 99% during school hours.
   - Support response: 1 business day for Tier 1.
   - These are achievable on Railway with the current
     architecture; if they're missed, dashboards will say so
     before users complain.

5. Ongoing operations rhythm
   - First half-term:
     * Monday: review weekend alerts.
     * Wednesday: check subscription health and device
       heartbeats.
     * Friday: triage feedback.
   - After settling:
     * Monthly: review retention job outputs, rotate
       secrets if due, scan audit log for anomalies.

6. Roadmap seeding (not built this phase)
   - Recurring bookings from the portal.
   - Catering / AV request integration.
   - Usage analytics for facilities.
   - Teams app surface for the mobile/Teams users.
   - Anything wave 1 feedback genuinely demands.


Exit criteria
-------------
- Every bookable room has the lockdown applied, an active
  Graph subscription, and (where appropriate) a working iPad
  that's been live for 7+ days.
- SLOs are met for two consecutive weeks after wave 2
  completes.
- IT team owns day-to-day operations; dev team is
  involved only for code changes and Tier 3 incidents.
- A retro document exists and its actions are tracked.


Risks and mitigations
---------------------
- Rolling out at the start of a term maximises visibility but
  also maximises blast radius. Mitigation: do wave 1 the week
  before term starts.
- Staff resistance to losing the Outlook-invite workflow is
  the biggest single risk in this rollout. We have removed
  that option deliberately, and there's no going back without
  unwinding the mailbox lockdown — which would undermine the
  permission model for the rooms that need it.
  Mitigation: communicate clearly; train well; make the
  portal obviously better; expect a couple of weeks of
  grumbling.
- Student usage may be much lower than staff and that's fine;
  we don't need to "drive adoption" for students — the
  rooms they can book are typically optional convenience
  bookings.
- Hardware failures at scale. Mitigation: hold 1-2 spare
  iPads pre-configured for swap.
- End-of-term freeze may forbid deployments in exam periods.
  Mitigation: plan around the academic calendar from day one.


What "done" looks like
----------------------
- Staff stop asking "is the system working?" and just use it.
- Students can find a study room and book it without
  thinking.
- Outlook invites to rooms get auto-declined and nobody is
  surprised by it.
- The dev team has moved on to the next thing.
